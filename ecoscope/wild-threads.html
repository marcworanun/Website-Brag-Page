Wild Threads

Some of the world’s most remarkable animals are quietly disappearing, from the forests of Thailand to the steppes of Central Asia. Each piece in this collection is made for one of them.

Clothes are seen every day, by everyone who passes the person wearing them. We use that to speak for animals that are running out of time, in Thailand and beyond. Every piece in Wild Threads is designed around a single endangered species, so wearing it carries that animal’s story out into the street.

Below, each animal sits beside the piece made for it, with what makes it remarkable, why it is in danger, and its status on the IUCN Red List — the global record of how close a species is to extinction.

Indian elephant

Elephas maximus indicus

IUCN Red List status

Endangered

  1. LC
  2. NT
  3. VU
  4. EN
  5. CR
  6. EW
  7. EX

Listed as Endangered since 1986.

The animal

The Indian elephant is the mainland subspecies of the Asian elephant, found from India across to Thailand and the rest of mainland Southeast Asia. It is Thailand’s national animal, and for centuries it has been woven into the country’s history, religion and royal tradition.

Elephants live in close family herds led by the oldest female, and carry their calves for up to 22 months — longer than any other mammal. Eating up to 150 kilograms of plants a day, they scatter seeds and open paths through the forest as they go, shaping the land that countless other species depend on.

Why it is endangered

A century ago, Thailand was home to an estimated 100,000 elephants. Today only around 4,000 live in its wild forests, and about 50,000 remain in the wild across the whole of Asia.

Their forests have been cleared for farms, plantations and roads, cutting herds off from one another and pushing them into fields and villages, where clashes with people turn deadly on both sides. Poaching adds to the loss: only some males grow tusks, and hunting them for ivory has made tuskers rarer still. Calves have also been taken from the wild to supply the tourism trade.

The piece

The Jaeson Pants carry the elephant in their cut. The crescent of lighter cloth set into each knee is shaped like a tusk — the ivory that has cost so many elephants their lives.

02

Przewalski’s horse

Equus ferus przewalskii

IUCN Red List status

Endangered

  1. LC
  2. NT
  3. VU
  4. EN
  5. CR
  6. EW
  7. EX

Listed as Extinct in the Wild until 2008.

The animal

Przewalski’s horse is a stocky wild horse of the Central Asian steppe, with a sandy dun coat, a pale muzzle and a short, upright mane that stands like a brush. Known in Mongolia as the takhi, it lives in small family bands led by a single stallion, and carries 66 chromosomes where a domestic horse has 64.

It is named after Nikolai Przhevalsky, the Russian explorer who brought it to the attention of European science in the late 1800s. Every Przewalski’s horse alive today descends from about a dozen animals caught in the wild around 1900.

Why it is endangered

Hunting, harsh winters and competition with livestock for water and grazing drove the horse from its last refuges in Mongolia and China. The last wild one was seen in 1969, and for decades the species survived only in zoos.

Since 1992, zoo-bred horses have been returned to the steppes of Mongolia, and later to China and Kazakhstan, and wild herds have slowly grown again. But they remain small and fragile: with so few founders the horses share a narrow gene pool, and a single severe winter can wipe out much of a herd, as one did in Mongolia in 2009 and 2010.

The piece

The Equus Long Sleeve takes its name from the genus every horse belongs to, and carries a watercolour of horses running free across the chest — the way the takhi once roamed the steppe, and roams again today.
