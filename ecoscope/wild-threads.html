Wild Threads

Some of the world’s most remarkable animals are quietly disappearing, from the forests of Thailand to the steppes of Central Asia. Each piece in this collection is made for one of them.

Clothes are seen every day, by everyone who passes the person wearing them. We use that to speak for animals that are running out of time, in Thailand and beyond. Every piece in Wild Threads is designed around a single endangered species, so wearing it carries that animal’s story out into the street.

Below, each animal sits beside the piece made for it, with what makes it remarkable, why it is in danger, and its status on the IUCN Red List — the global record of how close a species is to extinction.

01

Indian elephant

Elephas maximus indicus

IUCN Red List status

Endangered

  1. LC
  2. NT
  3. VU
  4. EN
  5. CR
  6. EW
  7. EX

Listed as Endangered since 1986.

The animal

The Indian elephant is Thailand’s national animal and the mainland subspecies of the Asian elephant. Herds are led by the oldest female, calves are carried for up to 22 months, and elephants spread seeds across the forest as they feed.

Why it is endangered

A century ago Thailand had an estimated 100,000 elephants; around 4,000 now live in the wild. Forests cleared for farms and roads push herds into villages, where clashes turn deadly, and males are poached for their tusks.

The crescent of lighter cloth on each knee of the Jaeson Pants is shaped like a tusk.

02

Przewalski’s horse

Equus ferus przewalskii

IUCN Red List status

Endangered

  1. LC
  2. NT
  3. VU
  4. EN
  5. CR
  6. EW
  7. EX

Listed as Extinct in the Wild until 2008.

The animal

A stocky wild horse of the Central Asian steppe, known in Mongolia as the takhi. Every Przewalski’s horse alive today descends from about a dozen caught in the wild around 1900.

Why it is endangered

Hunting, harsh winters and competition with livestock for grazing drove it from the wild; the last wild one was seen in 1969. Herds returned to Mongolia since 1992 are growing, but they remain small, closely related and at the mercy of severe winters.

The piece

Named after the horse genus, the Equus Long Sleeve carries a watercolour of horses running free.

03

Siamese crocodile

Crocodylus siamensis

IUCN Red List status

Critically Endangered

  1. LC
  2. NT
  3. VU
  4. EN
  5. CR
  6. EW
  7. EX

Listed as Critically Endangered since 1996.

The animal

Named after Siam, Thailand’s old name, this freshwater crocodile grows to around three metres and lives in slow rivers, swamps and lakes. Females lay their eggs in mounds of plants and guard the nest.

Why it is endangered

Hunted for its skin and taken from the wild to stock crocodile farms, it has almost vanished from Thailand’s rivers as wetlands were drained and dammed. Fewer than 1,000 adults are thought to survive in the wild, most of them in Cambodia.

The piece

The Siamese T Shirt curls a crocodile around the ECS emblem on its chest.

04

Sunda pangolin

Manis javanica

IUCN Red List status

Critically Endangered

  1. LC
  2. NT
  3. VU
  4. EN
  5. CR
  6. EW
  7. EX

Listed as Critically Endangered since 2014.

The animal

The Sunda pangolin lives in forests across Southeast Asia, Thailand included. Like every pangolin it is covered in scales made of keratin, the same material as our fingernails, has no teeth, and curls into a tight ball when threatened.

Why it is endangered

Pangolins are thought to be the most trafficked mammals in the world, hunted for their meat and for scales used in traditional medicine. International trade in them has been banned since 2017, yet smuggling continues.

The piece

The Pangolin Wallet’s textured cover is layered like a pangolin’s scales.
